An analytics dashboard an ops team can actually read

01 — The problem

Everything was on screen. Nothing was findable.

[DO UZUPEŁNIENIA — co się działo w firmie, kto zgłosił problem i dlaczego akurat wtedy.]

The dashboard showed every metric the database could produce. That felt thorough and worked terribly. People scrolled past twenty numbers to reach the one they check every morning.

"I know the number is in there. I just can't find it before the call starts."
[DO UZUPEŁNIENIA — prawdziwy cytat użytkownika albo usuń ten blok]

02 — What I found

I watched people use it before I changed anything.

[DO UZUPEŁNIENIA — jak badałeś: ile rozmów, obserwacji, jakie dane z analityki.] Three things came back every time.

  1. Everyone opened it for one number first

    Different roles, different number. The layout treated all of them as equally important, so none of them were.

  2. The numbers were trusted less than a spreadsheet

    Nothing said where a figure came from or when it was last updated, so people exported to Excel and checked by hand.

  3. The urgent and the routine looked identical

    A red alert and a stable metric had the same visual weight. Problems got noticed late.

03 — The system underneath

Before the layout, I mapped what feeds what.

Every metric on this screen comes from somewhere, updates on its own schedule, and means something different depending on who's looking. I mapped that first: sources, refresh rates, roles, and the decisions each role has to make.

That map is the reason the final layout looks obvious. The hierarchy on screen is the hierarchy of the work.

04 — The calls I made

Three decisions, and what each one cost.

Every one of these made something else worse. That's the job.

One screen per role, not one screen for all

Why

The number you open the dashboard for depends on your job. Ranking metrics for an average user served nobody.

The trade-off

More views to maintain. We agreed it was worth it for [DO UZUPEŁNIENIA — ile ról].

Every number says where it came from

Why

Trust was the real blocker. A source and a timestamp did more for adoption than any chart ever would.

The trade-off

More labels on screen, which fights the goal of calming the layout down.

Alerts get their own space

Why

Anything needing action today had to be impossible to scroll past.

The trade-off

Prime screen real estate sits empty on a good day. That emptiness is the signal.
