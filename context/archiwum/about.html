I came into design through systems. It shows.

The short version is on the home page. This is the longer one, for anyone deciding whether to put me in front of their product.

The long version

I read a product as a set of dependencies, not a stack of screens waiting to be painted. Where does this data come from, who is allowed to touch it, what breaks if it arrives late — that's the part I want to understand before I open Figma.

[DO UZUPEŁNIENIA — jak trafiłeś do designu: skąd przyszedłeś, co robiłeś wcześniej, co Cię przekonało. Dwa, trzy zdania. To jest miejsce, w którym rekruter zaczyna Cię lubić albo zamyka kartę.]

Give me a lot of data and very little room for it, and I'm in my element. Dense tables, five user roles, permissions that contradict each other, an edge case nobody documented — that's a good day, not a bad one.

I'm also easy to work with, which I mention because plenty of designers aren't. I explain my reasoning, I change my mind when the evidence says so, and I don't defend a layout because I spent three days on it.

What you get

Three things I'm reliably good at.

Flexible

New domain, messy brief, a stack I've never touched — fine. I ask until the picture is clear, then work with what's actually there instead of what I wish was there.

Data-heavy systems

Dashboards, admin panels, internal tools. The products with too many numbers and not enough room. Most designers avoid them. I ask for them.

Systems-first thinking

I design the rules, not just the screens. Components, states and logic that hold together after I've moved on to the next thing.

Off the clock

Board games, video games, and interfaces that disappear.

Right now I'm reading about UX in games. A game interface has to be invisible and lead the player by the hand at the same time — a contradiction that product design usually solves with a tooltip and hopes for the best.

I keep stealing from it. Onboarding that teaches by doing, feedback that arrives in the same second as the action, difficulty that ramps instead of dropping you in. None of that is games-only.
