## case-study-agyle.html

### Rozdział „The product" (zdjęty 2026-11-03)

Był pierwszym rozdziałem pod nagłówkiem strony, zanim strona przeszła na układ
modułowy. Treść ogólna o produkcie — może wrócić jako wstęp, jako akapit
w nagłówku strony albo na stronie „About".

> **Nadtytuł:** The product
>
> **H2:** A patient record is not an app. It is the floor everybody stands on.
>
> Agyle is a modular EPR deployed in NHS hospitals: secure management and
> analysis of large volumes of patient data, real-time insight, and clinical
> decisions made faster because the thing you need is already on screen.
> Mobile-first, because a lot of the people using it are standing up.
>
> That framing matters more than it sounds. Consumer products get to compete for
> attention; this one competes with a corridor. Every screen is read by somebody
> who is mid-task, often interrupted, and accountable for what happens next.
> Design decisions here are measured in seconds saved per interaction,
> multiplied by a department.
>
> Over four years at Fortrus I delivered five Agyle modules. Each was scoped and
> run as its own project, so the constant wasn't the process — it was the
> obligation to make five separately-built things feel like one system to the
> person using them.

### Rozdział „How the work ran" (zdjęty 2026-11-03)

Drugi rozdział wstępu. Trzeci akapit (Agyle Health Design System) jest wart
osobnego modułu — design system to materiał na własny rozdział, nie na wtrącenie.

> **Nadtytuł:** How the work ran
>
> **H2:** Five projects, five delivery shapes, one system to answer to.
>
> Direction came from wherever the module came from — Product Managers on some,
> Technical Directors on others — so the approach changed per project and
> priorities moved underneath us. I worked alongside Project Managers, Technical
> Consultants and NHS Business Strategists, in workshops, design reviews and
> solution presentations, which is where most of the real decisions got made.
>
> One thing never changed: close contact with developers and PMs for the whole
> length of a project, not a handover at the end. In a domain where an edge case
> is a patient, the cheapest place to catch a wrong assumption is a
> conversation, not a release.
>
> The thread tying it together was the Agyle Health Design System, which I led in
> a senior role. Standardised UI components and patterns, written for designers
> and developers at the same time, so a module built by a different squad in a
> different quarter still behaved like Agyle. It was the only way five parallel
> projects could stop producing five dialects.
